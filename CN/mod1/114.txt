114. Explain packet-switched networks and evaluate their total delay time.

Packet switching divides a message into packets and transmits them over shared network links.

Types:
1. Datagram network: Packets are routed independently.
2. Virtual-circuit network: Packets follow an established logical path.

Four types of delay:
1. Processing delay (d\_{proc}): Time to examine the packet.
2. Queuing delay (d\_{queue}): Time spent waiting in a buffer.
3. Transmission delay (d\_{trans}): Time to transmit all packet bits.
4. Propagation delay (d\_{prop}): Time for the signal to travel across the link.

Total delay:[d\_{total}=d\_{proc}+d\_{queue}+d\_{trans}+d\_{prop}]

Transmission delay:[d\_{trans}=\frac{L}{R}]

Propagation delay:[d\_{prop}=\frac{D}{V}]

Where (L) is packet size in bits, 
(R) is transmission rate, 
(D) is distance, and (V) is propagation speed.
