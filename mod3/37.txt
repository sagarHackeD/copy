37. Explain the Heart-Lung Machine.
The Heart-Lung Machine (Cardiopulmonary Bypass - CPB machine) is a medical device that temporarily takes over the function of the heart (pumping blood) and the lungs (oxygenating blood) during cardiac surgery. It allows surgeons to perform open-heart surgeries on a still, bloodless field.
Textual Diagram of the Heart-Lung Machine Circuit:
```
[Venous Cannula]  -->  [Venous Reservoir] --> [Oxygenator] --> [Heat Exchanger] --> [Arterial Filter] --> [Arterial Cannula]
 (Patient's Vena   |        (Gravity/          |    (Membrane)    |    (Heats/Cools   |      (Removes       |  (Returns to
  Cava/Right Atrium)|        Suction)           |  (Gas Exchange)  |     blood to      |       air bubbles    |   Aorta)
                    |                           |                  |    normothermia)  |      & debris)       |
                    |       [Cardiotomy Suction]|                  |                   |                       |
                    |       (Recovers shed      |                  |                   |                       |
                    |        blood from field)  |                  |                   |                       |
```
Main Components and Their Functions:
1.  Venous Cannula: A tube inserted into the vena cava or right atrium to drain deoxygenated blood from the patient's body by gravity or vacuum-assisted venous drainage.
2.  Venous Reservoir: A collection chamber that holds the blood before it is pumped. It provides a volume buffer and acts as a bubble trap.
3.  Oxygenator (The "Lung"): The most critical component.
    - Current Standard: Microporous hollow-fiber membrane oxygenators.
    - Function: Blood flows through hollow fibers while oxygen gas flows around them. Oxygen diffuses into the blood, and carbon dioxide (CO_2) diffuses out of the blood into the gas phase, effectively "breathing" for the patient.
4.  Heat Exchanger: Integrated with the oxygenator. Heats or cools the blood to manage the patient's body temperature (hypothermia is often induced to protect organs during surgery).
5.  Arterial Pump (The "Heart"): Usually a centrifugal pump (or roller pump). It propels the oxygenated blood back to the patient. It generates the pressure needed for systemic perfusion.
6.  Arterial Filter: An inline filter to capture microemboli (air bubbles, debris, clots) before the blood returns to the patient's aorta, preventing strokes.
7.  Cardiotomy Suction: Sucks up blood pooling in the surgical field and returns it to the reservoir.
Clinical Applications:
- Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting (CABG).
- Valve replacement/repair (Mitral, Aortic).
- Congenital heart defect repairs.
- Aortic aneurysm repair.
- Heart transplantation and mechanical circulatory support bridge procedures.
