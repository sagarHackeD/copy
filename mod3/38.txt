38. Define ECG and explain its significance.
Definition:
An Electrocardiogram (ECG or EKG) is a non-invasive diagnostic tool that records the electrical activity of the heart over a period of time. By placing surface electrodes on the skin (limbs and chest), it detects the tiny electrical changes that occur on the skin surface caused by the depolarization and repolarization of the cardiac muscle during each heartbeat.
The ECG Waveform (Textual Diagram):
```
        R (Peak)
       /\
      /  \
     /    \         T (Ventricular Repolarization)
    /      \       /\
   /        \_____/  \
  / P (Atrial  \      \
 /  Depolarization)   \
/                       \__________________________________
  Q   S (Ventricular Depolarization - QRS Complex)
```
Interpretation of the Normal ECG Complex:
| Wave/Interval | What it Represents | Clinical Significance |
|
| P Wave | Atrial depolarization (spread of electrical impulse through the atria). | Timing and morphology indicate atrial enlargement or conduction issues (e.g., atrial fibrillation). |
| PR Interval | Time from the start of atrial depolarization to the start of ventricular depolarization (includes AV node delay). | Prolonged > 200ms = First-degree heart block; shorter can indicate pre-excitation (Wolff-Parkinson-White). |
| QRS Complex | Ventricular depolarization (contraction). The Q wave (septal), R wave (main ventricular mass), S wave (late ventricular). | Broad QRS (>120ms) = Ventricular conduction delay (bundle branch block). Pathological Q waves indicate a previous heart attack (myocardial infarction). |
| ST Segment | The period between ventricular depolarization and repolarization (plateau phase - electrical silence). | ST Elevation = Acute MI (STEMI - heart attack). ST Depression = Ischemia or ventricular hypertrophy. |
| T Wave | Ventricular repolarization (relaxation). | Peaked T waves = Hyperkalemia (high potassium). Flat/inverted T waves = Ischemia or electrolyte imbalance. |
| QT Interval | Entire duration of ventricular electrical activity (from depolarization to repolarization). | Prolonged QT (e.g., >450ms) = Risk of fatal arrhythmias (Torsades de Pointes) due to genetic or drug-induced factors. |
Significance of ECG in Medicine:
1.  Diagnosis of Arrhythmias: Detects atrial fibrillation, ventricular tachycardia, bradycardias, and heart blocks.
2.  Detection of Myocardial Ischemia and Infarction: Crucial for diagnosing heart attacks (STEMI/NSTEMI) in emergency departments.
3.  Monitoring Electrolyte Disturbances: High or low K⁺, Ca²⁺, and Mg²⁺ levels alter the ECG waveform.
4.  Assessing Cardiac Hypertrophy: Detects enlarged heart chambers (e.g., Left Ventricular Hypertrophy - LVH).
5.  Evaluating Pacemaker Function: Checks if artificial pacemakers are capturing and sensing the heart correctly.
6.  Preoperative Assessment: Screens for occult cardiac disease before major surgeries.
