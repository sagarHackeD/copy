34. Explain the mechanism of filtration in the human kidney.
The kidney's filtration mechanism occurs in the nephron (the functional unit). It involves three main processes: Glomerular Filtration, Tubular Reabsorption, and Tubular Secretion.
Textual Diagram of a Nephron:
```
   [Afferent Arteriole] --> [Glomerulus (Capillary Tuft)]
              |                      |
        [Bowman's Capsule] <--- [Efferent Arteriole]
              |                      |
         (FILTRATION)                |
              |                      |
   [Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)]  ---> (REABSORPTION: Glucose, H2O, Na+, Cl-)
              |
   [Loop of Henle (Descending & Ascending)] ---> (Water & Ion concentration)
              |
   [Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT)] ---> (SECRETION & Reabsorption: K+, H+, pH balance)
              |
   [Collecting Duct] ---> (Final concentration of Urine, regulated by ADH)
              |
           [Urine]
```
Detailed Mechanism:
1.  Glomerular Filtration (Ultrafiltration):
    - Location: Glomerulus (a tuft of capillaries) within Bowman's capsule.
    - Driving Force: High hydrostatic pressure (created by the difference in diameter between the wider afferent and narrower efferent arterioles).
    - Filtration Barrier: The capillary endothelium, basement membrane, and podocyte foot processes form a selective filter.
    - What passes: Water, glucose, salts (Na⁺, K⁺), amino acids, urea, and small molecules.
    - What is retained: Blood cells, platelets, and large proteins (e.g., albumin). The filtrate (primary urine) is essentially protein-free plasma. ~180 liters are filtered per day.
2.  Tubular Reabsorption (Selective Recovery):
    - Location: Primarily the Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT), but also the Loop of Henle and DCT.
    - Mechanism: Active and passive transport of essential substances back into the peritubular capillaries.
    - What is reabsorbed: ~99% of water, 100% of glucose and amino acids, and most ions (Na⁺, Cl⁻, HCO_3⁻). This is where the body reclaims what it needs. In the Loop of Henle, the counter-current multiplier creates a concentration gradient in the medulla, allowing for water reabsorption later.
3.  Tubular Secretion (Waste Addition):
    - Location: Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT) and Collecting Duct.
    - Mechanism: Active transport of additional waste products from the blood into the tubular fluid.
    - What is secreted: H⁺ ions (acid-base balance), K⁺ ions (potassium regulation), and drugs/toxins (e.g., penicillin, creatinine). This ensures these substances are removed from the blood and excreted in urine.
4.  Excretion:
    - The final concentrated fluid (urine) passes through the collecting ducts to the renal pelvis, ureter, and bladder.
