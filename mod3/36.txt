36. Explain the heart as a pump system.
The heart is a powerful, dual-action, four-chambered muscular pump that circulates blood continuously through the pulmonary and systemic circuits.
Textual Diagram of the Heart Pump:
```
                    [Superior Vena Cava]
                           |
    [Pulmonary Artery] <--[Right Atrium]--> [Aorta]
       (To Lungs)        /         \        (To Body)
                        /           \
                 [Tricuspid]   [Mitral/Bicuspid]
                        \           /
       [Right Ventricle] \         / [Left Ventricle]
       (Pulmonary Pump)   [Ventricular Septum] (Systemic Pump)
                        /           \
                 [Pulmonary]   [Aortic]
                  (Valve)       (Valve)
                    |               |
                [To Lungs]      [To Body]
```
Structural Design of the Pump:
| Component | Function in the Pump System |
|
| Right Atrium (RA) | Receiving Chamber: Collects deoxygenated blood returning from the body via the SVC/IVC. |
| Right Ventricle (RV) | Pulmonary Pump: Pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs via the pulmonary artery. Generates low pressure (~25 mmHg). |
| Left Atrium (LA) | Receiving Chamber: Collects oxygenated blood returning from the lungs via the pulmonary veins. |
| Left Ventricle (LV) | Systemic Pump: Pumps oxygenated blood to the entire body via the aorta. Generates high pressure (~120 mmHg). Has the thickest muscular wall. |
| Valves (Check Valves) | Prevent Backflow: Tricuspid (RA→RV), Mitral/Bicuspid (LA→LV), Pulmonary (RV→Lungs), Aortic (LV→Aorta). |
| Septum | Structural Wall: Separates the right (oxygen-poor) and left (oxygen-rich) sides, preventing mixing of blood. |
Mechanism of the Pump Cycle (Cardiac Cycle):
1.  Diastole (Filling Phase): The atria and ventricles relax. The AV valves (Tricuspid and Mitral) open, allowing blood to passively flow from the atria into the ventricles. Atrial systole (atrial kick) adds the final ~20% of ventricular filling.
2.  Systole (Pumping Phase): The ventricles contract. Pressure spikes, forcing the AV valves closed (preventing backflow into atria). Once ventricular pressure exceeds aortic/pulmonary pressure, the semilunar valves open, and blood is ejected into the great arteries.
Key Performance Metrics:
- Cardiac Output (CO): Volume of blood pumped per minute (Normal ~5 L/min). CO = Heart Rate (HR) × Stroke Volume (SV).
- Frank-Starling Law: The heart automatically pumps more blood if it is stretched by increased venous return (intrinsic regulation).
