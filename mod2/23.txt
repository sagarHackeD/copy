23. Differentiate between PLA and PHA bioplastics.
| Feature | PLA (Polylactic Acid) | PHA (Polyhydroxyalkanoates) |
|
| Source & Production | Made from fermented plant starches (e.g., corn, sugarcane) through a chemical polymerization process. | Produced by microorganisms (bacteria) through fermentation. Bacteria store PHA as intracellular carbon/energy granules. |
| Raw Material | Plant-based sugars and starches. | Plant oils, sugars, or even waste streams fed to bacteria. |
| Biodegradability | Biodegradable but requires industrial composting (high heat, specific conditions) to break down efficiently. | Readily biodegradable in natural environments including soil, freshwater, and marine settings. |
| Mechanical Properties | Rigid, brittle, and has poor heat resistance (low glass transition temperature). | More flexible, durable, and has a wider range of mechanical properties (from rigid to elastomeric). |
| Production Cost | Relatively lower cost due to established manufacturing processes. | Higher cost due to complex bacterial fermentation and purification processes. |
| Common Applications | Packaging (cups, containers), 3D printing filaments, textiles, and medical sutures. | Medical implants, drug delivery, biodegradable straws, and agricultural films. |
