23. Explain requirements elicitation, its importance and the techniques used in requirements elicitation.
Definition
Requirements elicitation is the process of discovering, gathering and understanding the needs, expectations and constraints of stakeholders for a proposed software system.
It is an important activity within requirements engineering.
Importance of requirements elicitation
1. Identifies user needs: Helps developers understand what users expect from the system.
2. Reduces ambiguity: Clarifies incomplete or conflicting requirements.
3. Reduces development cost: Detects misunderstandings before they become expensive design or coding changes.
4. Defines project scope: Establishes what the software should and should not do.
5. Improves software quality: Helps ensure that important functions and constraints are identified.
6. Supports planning: Provides information for cost estimation, scheduling and resource allocation.
7. Improves stakeholder satisfaction: Involves users in defining the product.
Techniques used in requirements elicitation
1.Interviews
Developers ask stakeholders structured or open-ended questions.
- Advantages: Detailed information and clarification.
- Limitation: Time-consuming and dependent on the interviewer's skills.
2.Questionnaires and surveys
A set of questions is distributed to many users.
- Advantages: Economical and suitable for collecting responses from large groups.
- Limitation: Responses may lack detail.
3.Observation
The analyst watches users perform their actual tasks.
- Advantages: Reveals practical workflows and hidden requirements.
- Limitation: Users may behave differently when observed.
4.Workshops and brainstorming
Stakeholders and developers meet to discuss needs and generate ideas.
- Advantages: Encourages collaboration and can resolve conflicting expectations.
- Limitation: Requires effective facilitation.
5.Document analysis
Existing forms, manuals, policies, reports and legacy-system documentation are examined.
- Advantages: Helps understand existing business rules and processes.
- Limitation: Documents may be outdated or incomplete.
6.Prototyping
A preliminary version of the system is developed to obtain feedback.
- Advantages: Helps users clarify requirements by seeing and interacting with a proposed interface.
- Limitation: Users may mistake the prototype for a finished system.
7.Use cases and scenarios
Typical interactions between users and the system are described step by step.
- Advantages: Clarifies system behavior and user goals.
- Limitation: May not capture every non-functional requirement.
Example: For a college attendance system, the analyst may interview lecturers, observe attendance recording, study existing registers and build a prototype for feedback.
Conclusion: Requirements elicitation helps establish an accurate understanding of the system before detailed design and implementation begin.
