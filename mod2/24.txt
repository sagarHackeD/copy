24. Explain the properties and engineering applications of PHA.
Properties of PHA:
Polyhydroxyalkanoates (PHAs) are a family of microbial polyesters produced by bacteria, algae, and fungi as intracellular carbon and energy reserves. Their properties are highly tunable.
- Biodegradability: Fully biodegradable in various environments (soil, marine, compost) without leaving toxic residues.
- Biocompatibility: Non-toxic and compatible with living tissues, making them ideal for biomedical applications.
- Thermoplasticity: Can be melted and processed like conventional plastics.
- Tunable Mechanical Properties: Properties range from brittle and stiff (e.g., PHB) to flexible and elastomeric (e.g., PHO), depending on monomer composition.
- Piezoelectricity: Some PHAs exhibit piezoelectric properties, useful in sensors.
- UV Resistance: Excellent resistance to ultraviolet radiation.
Common Types of PHA:
- PHB (Poly-3-hydroxybutyrate): Stiff and brittle, most common.
- PHBV (Poly-3-hydroxybutyrate-co-3-hydroxyvalerate): More flexible and tougher than PHB.
- P4HB (Poly-4-hydroxybutyrate): Highly flexible; FDA-approved for some medical applications.
- PHBHHx (Poly-3-hydroxybutyrate-co-3-hydroxyhexanoate): Elastomer-like flexibility.
Engineering Applications of PHA:
| Application Area | Specific Uses | Key Properties Leveraged |
|
| Biomedical Engineering | Tissue engineering scaffolds, surgical sutures, wound dressings, drug delivery systems, artificial organs, and cardiovascular stents. | Biocompatibility, biodegradability, non-toxicity. |
| Packaging | Biodegradable films, coatings, straws, and cutlery. | Biodegradability in various environments, good barrier properties. |
| Agriculture | Biodegradable mulch films that decompose in the soil after harvest. | Soil biodegradability, eliminates plastic waste. |
| Textiles | Fibers and non-woven fabrics. | Biodegradable and can be processed like conventional polymers. |
| Automotive & Electronics | Interior components and casings (in development). | Renewable source and biodegradability. |
