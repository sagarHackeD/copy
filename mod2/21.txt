21. Explain the production of biodiesel from lipids along with its advantages and limitations.
Production of Biodiesel from Lipids:
Biodiesel is a renewable, biodegradable alternative to petroleum diesel, produced primarily through the transesterification of oils and fats into fatty acid methyl esters (FAME). Lipids (triglycerides) from various sources react with an alcohol (usually methanol) in the presence of a catalyst to produce biodiesel and glycerol as a byproduct.
Feedstocks for Lipid-Based Biodiesel:
- First Generation: Edible vegetable oils (palm, soybean, rapeseed).
- Second Generation: Non-edible oils, waste cooking oil, and animal fats.
- Third Generation: Microbial lipids from oleaginous microorganisms (yeast, algae, bacteria) grown on wastewater or other low-cost substrates.
Production Routes:
- Transesterification: The most common method, where triglycerides react with alcohol (catalyzed by base, acid, or enzyme) to form FAME and glycerol.
- Other Methods: Pyrolysis/thermal cracking, supercritical fluid method, and micro-emulsification.
Advantages of Biodiesel:
1.  Environmental Sustainability: Reduced greenhouse gas emissions and carbon neutrality.
2.  Biodegradability and Non-Toxicity: Less harmful to the environment in case of spills.
3.  Higher Cetane Number: Provides better ignition quality and combustion.
4.  Renewability: Produced from renewable biological sources.
5.  Waste Valorization: Can utilize waste cooking oil and industrial wastewater as feedstocks.
6.  Societal Benefits: Promotes rural development and local employment.
Limitations of Biodiesel:
1.  High Production Costs: Feedstock costs and processing expenses limit large-scale adoption.
2.  Feedstock Availability: Competition with food supply for edible oils; reliance on non-edible sources is still developing.
3.  Cold Flow Properties: Poor performance at low temperatures due to crystallization.
4.  Fuel Property Challenges: Higher free fatty acid (FFA) content in some feedstocks can interfere with the transesterification process.
5.  High Cost of Third-Generation Feedstocks: While promising, microbial lipid production still faces economic hurdles.
