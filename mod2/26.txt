26. Define biosensors and explain their principle, working, and applications.
Definition:
A biosensor is an analytical device that combines a biological recognition element (e.g., enzyme, antibody, nucleic acid) with a physicochemical transducer to detect a specific analyte and convert the biological response into a measurable signal.
Principle and Working:
The working principle of a biosensor involves three key steps:
1.  Recognition (Biorecognition Element): The biological element (e.g., enzyme, antibody) specifically interacts with the target analyte (substrate). This interaction is highly selective.
2.  Transduction (Transducer): The biological recognition event causes a physical or chemical change (e.g., change in mass, heat, pH, light, or electron flow). The transducer converts this change into a measurable electrical or optical signal.
3.  Signal Processing: The transducer's signal is amplified, processed, and displayed as a readable output (e.g., a digital reading on a screen).
Schematic Diagram:
```
[Analyte]  -->  [Biorecognition Element]  -->  [Transducer]  -->  [Signal Processor]  -->  [Display]
                  (Enzyme/Antibody/DNA)       (Electrochemical/      (Amplifier/
                                               Optical/etc.)         Microprocessor)
```
Types of Biosensors (based on transducer):
| Type | Principle | Common Applications |
|
| Electrochemical | Monitors changes in current, potential, or impedance. | Blood glucose monitoring, heavy metal detection. |
| Optical | Detects changes in light properties (fluorescence, absorbance). | Pathogen detection, DNA hybridization. |
| Piezoelectric | Measures mass changes via frequency shifts in a quartz crystal. | Bacterial detection, antigen-antibody interactions. |
| Thermal | Measures heat changes from biochemical reactions. | Enzymatic activity monitoring. |
| Field Effect Transistor (FET) | Senses changes in surface potential. | Viral load estimation, hormone detection. |
Applications of Biosensors:
1.  Medical Diagnostics: Glucose monitoring for diabetes, pregnancy tests, pathogen detection (e.g., COVID-19, HIV), and cancer biomarker detection.
2.  Food Safety: Detection of foodborne pathogens (e.g., *Salmonella*, *E. coli*), allergens, and toxins.
3.  Environmental Monitoring: Detection of heavy metals, pesticides, and water pollutants.
4.  Pharmaceutical & Drug Discovery: High-throughput screening of drug candidates and monitoring drug interactions.
