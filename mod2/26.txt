26. How can we validate requirements?
Definition
Requirements validation is the process of checking whether documented requirements accurately represent the customer's needs and are suitable for developing the intended software system.
It answers the question: “Are we specifying the right system?”
Objectives of requirements validation
1. Ensure that requirements meet customer needs.
2. Detect errors and omissions before development.
3. Identify ambiguous or conflicting requirements.
4. Confirm that requirements are feasible and testable.
5. Reduce costly changes during later development.
Characteristics of good requirements
Requirements should satisfy the following checks:
1.Validity: Do they represent the actual needs of stakeholders?
2.Consistency: Do any requirements contradict each other?
3.Completeness: Are all important functions, constraints and conditions included?
4.Feasibility: Can the requirements be implemented within the available technology, time and budget?
5.Unambiguity: Can the requirements be interpreted in only one reasonable way?
6.Verifiability: Can testing, inspection or analysis determine whether each requirement has been satisfied?
7.Traceability: Can each requirement be linked to its source and to the corresponding design, implementation and tests?
Techniques used for requirements validation
1.Requirements reviews
Stakeholders, analysts and developers examine the requirements document to identify mistakes, omissions and inconsistencies.
2.Prototyping
A preliminary model or interface is demonstrated to users so that they can confirm whether it reflects their needs.
3.Test-case generation
Test cases are developed from requirements. If a requirement cannot be tested clearly, it may be ambiguous or insufficiently specified.
4.Automated analysis
Tools can check requirements documents for duplication, missing fields, inconsistent terminology and certain types of ambiguity.
5.Model validation
Use case diagrams, data models and other representations are checked with stakeholders to ensure that they correctly describe system behavior.
Example
Consider the requirement:
“The banking application should respond quickly.”
This requirement is ambiguous because “quickly” is not measurable.
A better requirement would be:
“The banking application shall display the transaction status within two seconds for at least 95% of requests under the specified normal operating load.”
The revised requirement is more precise and can be verified through performance testing.
Difference between verification and validation
| Verification                                                                        | Validation                                                             |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Checks whether the product or specification conforms to its specified requirements. | Checks whether the requirements and resulting product meet user needs. |
| Asks: “Are we building the product right?”                                          | Asks: “Are we building the right product?”                             |
| Includes reviews and conformance checks.                                            | Includes stakeholder evaluation, prototyping and validation testing.   |
Conclusion: Requirements validation ensures that requirements are correct, complete, consistent, feasible and testable before they are used as the basis for software development.
