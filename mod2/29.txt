29. Explain DNA fingerprinting and its forensic applications.
DNA Fingerprinting:
DNA fingerprinting (or DNA profiling) is a biometric technique that analyzes specific, highly variable regions of an individual's DNA to create a unique genetic profile. Except for identical twins, every person's DNA contains distinct variations, enabling accurate identification.
The Principle of DNA Fingerprinting:
The technique exploits genetic polymorphism, particularly in non-coding regions of DNA that contain repetitive sequences. Two main types of repeats are used:
- Short Tandem Repeats (STRs): These are 2–6 base pair sequences repeated multiple times. The number of repeats varies greatly between individuals. STR analysis is the current gold standard.
- Variable Number Tandem Repeats (VNTRs): These are longer repeat sequences (10–60 bp) with even greater variability.
By analyzing multiple STR loci (e.g., 13 core loci in the U.S. CODIS database), a profile with a probability of match as low as 1 in 10^9 can be generated.
Workflow of DNA Fingerprinting:
1.  Sample Collection: Biological samples (blood, saliva, hair, semen, bone) are collected from the crime scene or individuals.
2.  DNA Extraction: DNA is isolated and purified from the cells in the sample using chemical or mechanical methods.
3.  PCR Amplification: Specific STR (or VNTR) regions are amplified exponentially using the Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR). This is crucial for generating enough DNA from trace samples.
4.  Fragment Analysis (Electrophoresis): The amplified DNA fragments are separated by size using capillary electrophoresis.
5.  Profile Generation & Comparison: The resulting pattern of DNA fragments (the "fingerprint") is analyzed and compared to reference samples to determine a match or exclusion.
Forensic Applications of DNA Fingerprinting:
| Application | Description |
|
| Criminal Investigation | Identifying perpetrators from trace evidence (blood, saliva, hair, skin cells) left at crime scenes. |
| Exoneration of the Innocent | DNA evidence has been used to overturn wrongful convictions. |
| Paternity & Maternity Testing | Determining biological relationships in legal disputes, child custody cases, and immigration. |
| Identification of Human Remains | Identifying victims of disasters, mass casualties, and war, as seen in the identification of war victims in Ukraine. |
| Missing Persons & Cold Cases | Matching DNA from unidentified remains to family members of missing persons. |
| Sexual Assault Cases | Identifying perpetrators from biological evidence in rape and murder cases. |
