27. Explain the role of enzymes in glucose biosensors.
The most common and successful example of a biosensor is the electrochemical glucose biosensor, widely used by diabetics. The enzyme glucose oxidase (GOx) plays the central role in this device.
The Role of Glucose Oxidase (GOx):
Glucose oxidase is an enzyme that specifically catalyzes the oxidation of β-D-glucose to D-glucono-δ-lactone and hydrogen peroxide (H_2O_2).
The Chemical Reaction:
```
Glucose + O_2  --(Glucose Oxidase)-->  Gluconic Acid + H_2O_2
```
Mechanism in the Biosensor:
1.  Selective Recognition: GOx acts as the biorecognition element. It is highly specific to glucose, ensuring that the sensor does not respond to other sugars (like fructose or galactose). This selectivity is crucial for accurate readings.
2.  Signal Generation: The enzyme "takes something that is difficult to measure—glucose—and creates something that is easy to measure—hydrogen peroxide". The amount of hydrogen peroxide produced is directly proportional to the concentration of glucose in the sample.
3.  Electrochemical Detection (Transduction): The hydrogen peroxide produced is electrochemically detected. In a typical amperometric glucose biosensor, the enzyme is immobilized on an electrode. When glucose is present, the produced H_2O_2 is oxidized at the electrode surface, generating a current. This current is proportional to the glucose concentration.
Alternative Mechanism (Glucose Dehydrogenase):
Some biosensors use glucose dehydrogenase (GDH) instead of GOx. GDH catalyzes the oxidation of glucose using a cofactor like NAD⁺ or a artificial electron mediator. The reduced cofactor (e.g., NADH) is then electrochemically detected.
Summary:
The enzyme (GOx) provides the specificity to recognize glucose, and its catalytic action converts the glucose into a product (H_2O_2) that can be easily transduced into an electrical signal by the electrode, allowing for accurate and rapid glucose measurement.
