25. Explain DNA vaccines and RNA vaccines. Compare DNA and RNA vaccines.
DNA Vaccines:
DNA vaccines consist of a small, circular piece of bacterial DNA (plasmid) that has been engineered to contain a gene encoding a specific antigen from a pathogen. When injected into the body, host cells take up the DNA, which then travels to the nucleus. Inside the nucleus, the DNA is transcribed into mRNA, which is then translated into the antigenic protein in the cytoplasm. This protein is then presented to the immune system, triggering both cellular and humoral immune responses.
RNA Vaccines:
RNA vaccines use synthetic messenger RNA (mRNA) that encodes the antigen of interest. The mRNA is typically encapsulated in lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) to protect it and facilitate delivery. Once injected, the mRNA enters the host cell's cytoplasm (it does not need to enter the nucleus) and is directly translated into the antigenic protein by the cell's ribosomes. The protein is then presented to the immune system, triggering a potent immune response.
Comparison of DNA and RNA Vaccines:
| Feature | DNA Vaccines | RNA Vaccines |
|
| Mechanism | DNA enters nucleus → transcribed to mRNA → translated to protein. | mRNA enters cytoplasm → directly translated to protein. |
| Delivery | Requires delivery to the nucleus (e.g., electroporation, gene gun). | Only needs to reach the cytoplasm; often delivered via lipid nanoparticles (LNPs). |
| Stability | Generally stable at 2–8°C; can be freeze-dried. | Requires ultracold storage (−20°C to −70°C) for stability. |
| Production | Relatively easy and low-cost to produce in large quantities. | Fast to produce but initially more expensive; scale-up is improving. |
| Immunogenicity | May require adjuvants to enhance the immune response. | Often has innate immunostimulatory properties; may not need additional adjuvants. |
| Safety | Low but potential risk of integration into the host genome. | No risk of integration; mRNA is degraded by normal cellular processes. |
| Efficacy | Generally induces a strong cellular and humoral response. | Potent inducer of both cellular and humoral responses, especially effective at stimulating T cells. |
