22. Explain PLA synthesis and its engineering applications.
PLA Synthesis:
Polylactic acid (PLA), also known as polylactide, is a biodegradable, biocompatible polymer derived from renewable resources like corn starch or sugarcane. Lactic acid is first produced by fermenting sugars, and then PLA is synthesized through two main routes:
1.  Direct Polycondensation: Lactic acid monomers are directly polymerized, but this method typically yields low molecular weight PLA.
2.  Ring-Opening Polymerization (ROP): This is the most common industrial method. Lactic acid is first converted into a cyclic dimer called lactide. The lactide is then polymerized via ROP using a metal catalyst (e.g., tin octoate) to produce high molecular weight PLA.
3.  Biotechnological Route: Enzymatic ring-opening polymerization (eROP) using enzymes like lipases is an emerging, greener alternative.
Engineering Applications of PLA:
| Application Area | Specific Uses | Key Properties Leveraged |
|
| Biomedical Engineering | Surgical sutures, drug delivery systems, tissue engineering scaffolds, and implants. | Biocompatibility, biodegradability, non-toxicity. |
| 3D Printing (Additive Manufacturing) | Filament for fused deposition modeling (FDM) printers. | Ease of processing, good printability, and biodegradability. |
| Sustainable Packaging | Compostable food containers, cups, bowls, bags, and films. | Renewable origin, biodegradability (under industrial composting), and food safety. |
| Textile Industry | Eco-friendly fibers for clothing and non-woven fabrics. | Renewable, biodegradable, and can be spun into fibers. |
| Agriculture | Biodegradable mulch films and plant pots. | Biodegradability eliminates the need for removal after use. |
