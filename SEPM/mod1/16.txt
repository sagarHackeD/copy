16. Explain the waterfall model along with its pros and cons.
Definition
The waterfall model is a linear, sequential software development model in which development progresses through a series of phases. Each phase is generally completed before the next phase begins.
Diagram
1.Requirements analysis
2.System and software design
3.Implementation and unit testing
4.Integration and system testing
5.Deployment and maintenance
Phases of the waterfall model
1.Requirements analysis: All user requirements are gathered, analyzed and documented in a requirements specification.
2.System and software design: The architecture, database, interfaces and software components are designed.
3.Implementation and unit testing: The design is converted into source code. Individual components are tested independently.
4.Integration and system testing: Components are combined and the complete system is tested against the specified requirements.
5.Deployment and maintenance: The software is delivered to users. Errors are corrected, and changes or enhancements are made as necessary.
Advantages
1. Simple and easy to understand.
2. Clear phases and milestones.
3. Easier to plan and monitor progress.
4. Documentation is produced at each major stage.
5. Suitable when requirements are stable and well understood.
Disadvantages
1. Difficult to accommodate changes once a phase is completed.
2. Working software is generally available late in development.
3. Customer feedback may arrive too late to influence earlier decisions.
4. Major problems may be discovered during later testing.
5. Not suitable for projects with highly uncertain requirements.
Example: A small software project with fixed, legally specified requirements may use a waterfall-style process if the requirements are well understood.
Conclusion: The waterfall model is appropriate for stable, clearly defined projects, but iterative approaches are often better when requirements change frequently.
