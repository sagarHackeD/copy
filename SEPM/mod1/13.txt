13. Explain software myths with examples.
Definition
Software myths are false beliefs about software development that appear reasonable but can lead to poor planning, unrealistic expectations and project failure.
Software myths are commonly classified into three categories.
1. Management myths
These are incorrect beliefs held by managers.
- Myth: Existing standards and procedures are sufficient for successful software development.
- Reality: Standards must be appropriate, complete and followed by a skilled team.
* Myth: Adding more programmers to a delayed project will make it finish sooner.
* Reality: New members require training and communication, which may delay the project further.
2. Customer myths
These are misconceptions held by customers.
- Myth: A general statement of objectives is enough to begin coding.
- Reality: Detailed requirements and clarification are necessary to avoid misunderstandings.
* Myth: Changes to requirements are easy because software is flexible.
* Reality: Late changes may affect design, code, testing, cost and delivery schedules.
3. Practitioner myths
These are misconceptions held by software developers.
- Myth: Once the program works, the job is complete.
- Reality: Testing, documentation, deployment and maintenance are also necessary.
* Myth: Software quality can be assessed only after execution.
* Reality: Reviews, inspections and static analysis can identify defects before execution.
| Type of myth | Example                                   | Reality                                  |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Management   | More staff always means faster delivery   | Communication overhead can increase      |
| Customer     | Requirements never need detailed analysis | Clear requirements reduce rework         |
| Practitioner | Working code means a finished product     | Testing and maintenance remain necessary |
Conclusion: Identifying software myths helps organizations make realistic decisions and develop better-quality software.
