44. Compare photosynthesis and photovoltaic cells.
Photosynthesis:
Photosynthesis is the biological process by which plants, algae, and certain bacteria convert sunlight into chemical energy. Photosynthetic cells contain pigment-protein complexes (Photosystems I and II) that absorb photons and transfer electrons, producing energy-rich compounds such as ATP and NADPH. These molecules then power the Calvin cycle, reducing atmospheric carbon dioxide into sugars and releasing oxygen.
Photovoltaic (PV) Cells:
Photovoltaic cells (solar panels) are man-made semiconductor devices that directly convert solar radiation into electricity. When sunlight hits the semiconductor material (usually silicon), it excites electrons, creating an electrical current.
Comparison Table:
| Feature | Photosynthesis | Photovoltaic Cells |
|
| Output | Chemical Energy (sugars, biomass). | Electrical Energy (electricity). |
| Mechanism | Light energy drives electron transfer in pigment-protein complexes, producing ATP and NADPH. | Light energy excites electrons in a semiconductor, creating a flow of electric current. |
| Energy Conversion Efficiency | Lower (~1-3% for most plants; up to ~6% for some algae). | Higher (15–22% for commercial panels; >40% for multi-junction cells). |
| Wavelength Utilization | Primarily absorbs visible light (400–700 nm), which is only ~43% of sunlight. | Can be engineered to absorb a broader spectrum, including UV and infrared. |
| Self-Repair | Yes – plants can repair damage and regenerate. | No – cells degrade over time and require replacement. |
| Carbon Footprint | Carbon Negative – absorbs CO_2 from the atmosphere. | Carbon Neutral during operation, but manufacturing has an environmental cost. |
| Storage | Energy is stored as chemical bonds (sugars, starch) for later use. | Energy must be stored in batteries or converted to other forms for later use. |
