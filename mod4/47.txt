47. Explain shark skin-inspired technology and frictionless swim suits.
Shark Skin Structure:
Shark skin is covered in tiny, tooth-like scales called dermal denticles. These denticles are not flat; they have raised ridges (riblets) that run in the direction of water flow.
Drag Reduction Mechanism:
- The riblets on shark denticles create tiny vortices that reduce the formation of larger, drag-inducing turbulent eddies as water flows over the skin.
- This reduces skin friction drag, allowing sharks to glide through water with remarkable efficiency.
Shark Skin-Inspired Swimsuits:
Engineers and designers at Speedo took inspiration from this natural design to create the Fastskin series of swimsuits.
Design Features of the Suits:
- Texture: The suit's surface is covered in tiny, ridge-like structures that mimic shark denticles.
- Material: Advanced polymers create a super-hydrophobic surface that repels water.
- Design: The suits are engineered to compress the swimmer's body into a more streamlined shape.
Impact and Controversy:
- In the 2008 Beijing Olympics, an unprecedented 23 world records were broken by athletes wearing these suits.
- This led to debates about "technological doping" and the fairness of the suits.
- In 2010, FINA (now World Aquatics) banned full-body swimsuits and placed restrictions on materials.
