12. Compare Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic cells.
| Feature | Prokaryotic Cell | Eukaryotic Cell |
|
| Definition | Unicellular organisms lacking a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles (e.g., Bacteria, Archaea). | Unicellular or multicellular organisms possessing a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles (e.g., Plants, Animals, Fungi, Protists). |
| Nucleus | Absent (nucleoid region – irregularly shaped area containing DNA). | Present (well-defined, membrane-bound nucleus). |
| Membrane-bound Organelles | Absent (only ribosomes). | Present (ER, Golgi, Mitochondria, Lysosomes, etc.). |
| Ribosomes | 70S (smaller; 50S + 30S subunits). | 80S (larger; 60S + 40S subunits). |
| Cell Wall | Present (usually made of *peptidoglycan* in bacteria; pseudomurein in archaea). | Present in plants/fungi (cellulose in plants, chitin in fungi); absent in animal cells. |
| DNA Structure | Circular, naked DNA (no histones). | Linear DNA associated with histone proteins (forming chromatin). |
| Organelles for Energy | No mitochondria or chloroplasts; respiration occurs via the plasma membrane (mesosomes). | Mitochondria (respiration); Chloroplasts (photosynthesis in plants). |
| Cell Division | Binary fission (simple division). | Mitosis (somatic) and Meiosis (gametes). |
| Size | Typically 0.1 – 5.0 µm. | Typically 10 – 100 µm (much larger). |
| Example | *E. coli*, *Streptococcus*. | Human liver cell, *Chlamydomonas*, Yeast. |
