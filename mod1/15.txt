15. Define biomolecules and explain their classification with examples.
Definition: Biomolecules are organic (and some inorganic) molecules that are produced by living cells and are essential for life processes. They form the structural and functional units of cells.
Classification:
A. Based on Molecular Weight:
1.  Micromolecules (Small molecules): Low molecular weight (< 1000 Da). E.g., Amino acids, Nucleotides, Sugars (glucose), Fatty acids, Vitamins, Water, Minerals.
2.  Macromolecules (Large polymers): High molecular weight (> 1000 Da). Formed by polymerization of micromolecules. E.g., Polysaccharides (starch), Proteins (hemoglobin), Nucleic acids (DNA), Lipids (though not true polymers, they are often included here).
B. Based on Chemical Composition and Function:
1.  Carbohydrates: (C,H,O) – Sugars, starches, cellulose. E.g., Glucose, Glycogen.
2.  Proteins: (C,H,O,N,S) – Polymers of amino acids. E.g., Enzymes, Hemoglobin, Collagen.
3.  Lipids: (C,H,O – mostly hydrophobic) – Fats, oils, phospholipids, steroids. E.g., Triglycerides, Cholesterol.
4.  Nucleic Acids: (C,H,O,N,P) – Polymers of nucleotides. E.g., DNA, RNA.
5.  Vitamins: Organic molecules required in small quantities for normal metabolism. E.g., Vitamin C, Vitamin B12.
6.  Inorganic Minerals: Ions like Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Cl⁻, PO_4³⁻. E.g., Calcium in bones, Iron in hemoglobin.
C. Based on Biological Role:
1.  Primary Metabolites: Directly involved in normal growth, development, and reproduction. E.g., Amino acids, Glucose, Nucleotides, ATP.
2.  Secondary Metabolites: Not directly essential for growth but provide survival advantages (defense, competition). E.g., Alkaloids (Morphine), Antibiotics (Penicillin), Pigments (Carotenoids), Toxins, Flavonoids.
