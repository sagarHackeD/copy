11. Tautology and truth table
Definition:  
A compound proposition is a tautology if it is true for every possible truth assignment of its component propositions.
We prove that  
[[(pV q)Λ(p→r)Λ(q→r)]→r]
is a tautology.
Truth table:
| (p) | (q) | (r) | (pV q) | (p→r) | (q→r) | (A=(pV q)Λ(p→r)Λ(q→r)) | (A→r) |
| T | T | T | T | T | T | T | T |
| T | T | F | T | F | F | F | T |
| T | F | T | T | T | T | T | T |
| T | F | F | T | F | T | F | T |
| F | T | T | T | T | T | T | T |
| F | T | F | T | T | F | F | T |
| F | F | T | F | T | T | F | T |
| F | F | F | F | T | T | F | T |
Since (A→r) is true in every row, the proposition is a tautology.
