16. Explain the properties and functions of carbohydrates.
General Properties:
- Empirical Formula: (CH_2O)_n (e.g., glucose C_6H_1_2O_6).
- Solubility: Monosaccharides and disaccharides are crystalline, water-soluble, and sweet-tasting. Polysaccharides are insoluble or form colloidal solutions.
- Optical Activity: Most carbohydrates are optically active (rotate plane-polarized light) due to chiral carbon atoms.
- Reducing vs. Non-reducing: Reducing sugars (e.g., glucose, maltose) have a free aldehyde or ketone group and can reduce Cu²⁺ to Cu⁺ (Benedict's test). Non-reducing sugars (e.g., sucrose) lack a free anomeric carbon.
- Isomerism: They exhibit structural and stereoisomerism (α and β forms).
Classification (Brief):
- Monosaccharides: Single sugar unit (e.g., Glucose, Fructose, Galactose).
- Disaccharides: Two units joined by a glycosidic bond (e.g., Sucrose = Glucose+Fructose; Maltose = Glucose+Glucose; Lactose = Glucose+Galactose).
- Polysaccharides: Polymers of many monosaccharides (e.g., Starch, Glycogen, Cellulose, Chitin).
Functions:
1.  Primary Energy Source: Glucose is the main fuel for cellular respiration (glycolysis, Krebs cycle) to generate ATP. 1 gram of carbohydrate yields ~4 kcal.
2.  Energy Storage: Excess glucose is stored as glycogen (in animals, in liver and muscles) and starch (in plants). Glycogen provides rapid glucose release between meals.
3.  Structural Support: Cellulose (plant cell walls) provides rigidity to plants. Chitin (exoskeleton of arthropods and fungal cell walls) provides structural strength.
4.  Cell-Cell Recognition & Signaling: Glycoproteins and glycolipids on the cell surface act as antigens (e.g., ABO blood group markers) and receptors for hormones.
5.  Precursor Molecules: Ribose and deoxyribose are essential components of nucleic acids (RNA and DNA).
6.  Dietary Fiber: Indigestible polysaccharides (cellulose, hemicellulose) promote bowel health by adding bulk to feces and preventing constipation.
7.  Spare Protein: Adequate carbohydrate intake prevents the breakdown of proteins for energy (protein-sparing effect).
