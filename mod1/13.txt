13. Explain stem cells, their types, properties, and applications.
Stem Cells are undifferentiated, unspecialized cells that have the remarkable ability to divide repeatedly (self-renew) and differentiate into specialized cell types under specific physiological or experimental conditions.
Properties:
1.  Self-renewal: They can undergo numerous mitotic divisions while maintaining their undifferentiated state.
2.  Potency: They can differentiate into one or more mature cell types.
3.  Plasticity: In certain conditions, they can trans-differentiate (switch from one lineage to another).
Types (Based on Potency):
| Type | Potency | Description | Example |
|
| Totipotent | Highest | Can form all embryonic and extra-embryonic tissues (entire organism). | Zygote, early blastomeres (up to 8-cell stage). |
| Pluripotent | Very High | Can form all cell types of the three germ layers (ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm) but *not* extra-embryonic tissues. | Embryonic Stem Cells (ESCs), Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells (iPSCs). |
| Multipotent | Limited | Can differentiate into a limited number of related cell types within a specific tissue or lineage. | Hematopoietic Stem Cells (HSCs) - form blood cells; Mesenchymal Stem Cells (MSCs). |
| Unipotent | Lowest | Can only differentiate into one specific cell type, but retain self-renewal. | Spermatogonial stem cells (form sperm). |
Applications:
1.  Regenerative Medicine: Treating Parkinson’s (replace dopamine neurons), Spinal cord injuries (replace neurons/glia), Type 1 Diabetes (replace pancreatic beta cells), and Heart disease (repair cardiac muscle).
2.  Drug Discovery & Testing: Testing new drugs for toxicity and efficacy on specific human cell types without using animal models.
3.  Disease Modeling: Creating iPSCs from patients with genetic disorders (e.g., Cystic Fibrosis) to study disease progression in a lab dish.
4.  Gene Therapy: Correcting defective genes in stem cells and reintroducing them to the patient (e.g., using CRISPR on HSCs for Sickle Cell Anemia).
5.  Bone Marrow Transplants: Transplantation of HSCs to treat leukemia and lymphoma (graft-versus-tumor effect).
