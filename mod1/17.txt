17. Explain the properties and functions of lipids.
Properties:
- Solubility: Lipids are hydrophobic (insoluble in water) but soluble in non-polar organic solvents like chloroform, ether, and benzene.
- Energy Density: They yield ~9 kcal per gram, more than double that of carbohydrates.
- Physical State: Fats are solid at room temperature (saturated, e.g., animal fats); oils are liquid at room temperature (unsaturated, e.g., plant oils).
- Amphipathic Nature: Phospholipids and glycolipids have both hydrophilic (polar) heads and hydrophobic (non-polar) tails, allowing them to form bilayers.
- Esterification: Fats and oils are esters formed from glycerol and fatty acids.
Classification:
- Simple Lipids: Esters of fatty acids and alcohols. E.g., Triglycerides (fats/oils), Waxes.
- Compound/Complex Lipids: Contain additional groups. E.g., Phospholipids (contain phosphate), Glycolipids (contain sugar).
- Derived Lipids: Hydrolysis products of simple/complex lipids. E.g., Steroids (cholesterol, hormones), Fatty acids, Glycerol.
- Isoprenoids/Terpenes: Built from isoprene units. E.g., Vitamin A, Carotenoids.
Functions:
1.  Energy Storage: Triglycerides stored in adipose tissue are the body's major form of long-term energy reserve.
2.  Structural Component of Membranes: Phospholipids and cholesterol form the phospholipid bilayer of cell membranes, providing fluidity, flexibility, and selective permeability.
3.  Insulation: Subcutaneous fat acts as a thermal insulator (prevents heat loss) and provides cushioning (protection) for internal organs.
4.  Signaling Molecules: Steroid hormones (testosterone, estrogen, cortisol) and eicosanoids (prostaglandins) act as chemical messengers regulating metabolism, reproduction, and inflammation.
5.  Vitamin Absorption: Lipids aid in the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) in the intestine.
6.  Protection: Waxes protect plant surfaces (cuticle) from water loss and animal skin/fur (e.g., earwax).
7.  Myelin Sheath: Lipids (sphingolipids) form the myelin sheath around neurons, enabling rapid electrical impulse conduction (saltatory conduction).
