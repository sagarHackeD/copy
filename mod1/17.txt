17. Explain bibliographic databases, Web of Science, Google and Google Scholar.

 1. Bibliographic databases

Bibliographic databases are organized collections of information about research publications, including titles, authors, abstracts, keywords, and references.

Uses:

- Finding relevant research papers.
- Tracking citations.
- Identifying important researchers.
- Studying research trends.

Examples include Scopus, IEEE Xplore, and Web of Science.

 2. Web of Science

Web of Science is a multidisciplinary research database that indexes selected scholarly publications and their citations.

Features:

- Citation tracking.
- Author and journal analysis.
- Research trend analysis.
- Identification of influential publications.

 3. Google

Google is a general-purpose search engine that searches a wide range of web content.

Features:

- Finds websites, technical reports, documentation, and articles.
- Supports keyword searches and advanced search operators.
- Provides broad access to technical information.

Limitation: Search results may include unreliable or non-scholarly sources.

 4. Google Scholar

Google Scholar is a search engine focused on scholarly literature.

Features:

- Finds research papers, theses, books, and conference proceedings.
- Displays citation counts.
- Provides related articles and versions of publications.
- Allows users to create author profiles and citation alerts.
