19. Explain the importance of special biomolecules.
*Interpretation: "Special biomolecules" here refers to specialized functional biomolecules that are not generic structural or energy-storage molecules, but rather molecules with highly specific, crucial roles in cellular and systemic physiology.*
1. Enzymes (Specialized Proteins):
- Importance: They act as biological catalysts, lowering the activation energy of biochemical reactions, increasing reaction rates by millions of times.
- Examples: DNA polymerase (replicates DNA), Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase (RuBisCO – fixes CO_2 in photosynthesis), Pepsin (digests proteins).
- Significance: Without enzymes, cellular metabolism would be too slow to sustain life.
2. Hormones (Chemical Messengers):
- Importance: They coordinate and regulate the physiological activities of different tissues and organs via long-distance signaling (endocrine).
- Examples: Insulin (lowers blood glucose), Glucagon (raises blood glucose), Thyroxine (regulates metabolic rate), Estrogen/Testosterone (regulate reproduction).
- Significance: Maintain homeostasis (e.g., blood sugar, calcium levels, thermoregulation).
3. Hemoglobin and Myoglobin (Oxygen Transport/Storage Proteins):
- Importance: Hemoglobin (in RBCs) transports oxygen from lungs to tissues and carries CO_2 back. Myoglobin (in muscles) stores oxygen for aerobic respiration.
- Significance: Enables efficient aerobic metabolism in complex, large-bodied animals. Defects (e.g., sickle cell anemia) disrupt this vital transport.
4. Chlorophyll and Carotenoids (Photosynthetic Pigments):
- Importance: Chlorophyll a and b absorb specific wavelengths of light (blue and red) and convert light energy into chemical energy (ATP and NADPH) in photosynthesis.
- Significance: They are the primary drivers of almost all food chains on Earth, converting solar energy into biomass and releasing oxygen.
5. Immunoglobulins (Antibodies):
- Importance: These are Y-shaped glycoproteins that specifically recognize and bind to foreign antigens (pathogens), neutralizing them or tagging them for destruction by immune cells (opsonization).
- Significance: They form the humoral arm of adaptive immunity, providing specific, long-term protection against infections.
6. ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate – a Nucleotide):
- Importance: It is the "energy currency" of the cell. It stores chemical energy in its high-energy phosphoanhydride bonds; hydrolysis to ADP + Pi releases energy for all cellular work (mechanical, chemical, transport).
- Significance: Couples exergonic (energy-releasing) reactions with endergonic (energy-requiring) processes, making life's continuous work possible.
7. Cytochromes (Electron Carriers):
- Importance: These are heme-containing proteins (found in the electron transport chain) that undergo reversible redox reactions, passing electrons from one carrier to the next.
- Significance: Essential for oxidative phosphorylation (ATP generation) in mitochondria and photosynthesis.
