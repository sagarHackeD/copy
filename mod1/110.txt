110. What are citations and attributions? Explain their functions and different styles of citations.

Citation: A citation is a reference in a research document that identifies the source of information, ideas, data, or quotations.

Attribution: Attribution means explicitly giving credit to the person or source responsible for an idea, statement, image, method, or other contribution.

 Functions of citations and attributions

1. Acknowledge the original author.
2. Avoid plagiarism.
3. Support claims with evidence.
4. Allow readers to locate original sources.
5. Demonstrate the depth of the literature review.
6. Distinguish original contributions from existing work.

 Different citation styles

| Style   | Format                                | Common use                                |
| ------- | ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| APA     | Author–date, e.g., (Sharma, 2024)     | Social sciences and some technical fields |
| IEEE    | Numbered, e.g., [1]                   | Engineering and technology                |
| MLA     | Author–page, e.g., (Sharma 25)        | Humanities                                |
| Chicago | Notes and bibliography or author–date | History and other disciplines             |
| Harvard | Author–date, e.g., (Sharma, 2024)     | Various academic fields                   |

Example of IEEE style:

In-text citation: Energy efficiency can be improved using smart monitoring systems [1].

Reference: `[1] A. Sharma, "Smart Energy Monitoring," Journal Name, vol. 5, no. 2, pp. 10–15, 2024.`
