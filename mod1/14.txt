14. Explain the structure, properties, and functions of nucleic acids with emphasis on DNA.
Structure:
Nucleic acids are polymers of nucleotides. A nucleotide has three components:
1.  A Nitrogenous Base (Purines: Adenine [A], Guanine [G]; Pyrimidines: Cytosine [C], Thymine [T] in DNA, Uracil [U] in RNA).
2.  A Pentose Sugar (Deoxyribose in DNA; Ribose in RNA).
3.  A Phosphate Group.
DNA Structure (Double Helix – Watson & Crick Model):
- Two long polynucleotide chains wound around each other in a right-handed helix.
- The sugar-phosphate backbones are on the outside, while the bases face inward.
- Base Pairing: A pairs with T (two hydrogen bonds); C pairs with G (three hydrogen bonds).
- The two strands are antiparallel (one runs 5'→3', the other 3'→5').
Properties of DNA:
1.  Stability: The double helix is highly stable due to base stacking (hydrophobic interactions) and H-bonds.
2.  Semi-conservative Replication: Each strand serves as a template for a new complementary strand.
3.  Heat Denaturation (Melting): When heated, the H-bonds break, separating the two strands (higher GC content = higher melting temperature due to 3 H-bonds).
4.  Negatively Charged: Due to the phosphate groups, DNA migrates toward the positive electrode in electrophoresis.
5.  UV Absorbance: DNA absorbs ultraviolet (UV) light maximally at ~260 nm.
Functions of Nucleic Acids (Emphasis on DNA):
1.  Genetic Storage (DNA): DNA is the hereditary material that stores all the genetic information required for the development, functioning, and reproduction of all living organisms.
2.  Replication: DNA can replicate itself precisely during cell division, ensuring genetic continuity.
3.  Gene Expression (Transcription): DNA serves as a template for the synthesis of RNA (mRNA, tRNA, rRNA).
4.  Protein Synthesis (Translation): Through mRNA, DNA dictates the sequence of amino acids in proteins.
5.  Mutation and Evolution: Changes in DNA sequences (mutations) provide genetic variation, which is the raw material for evolution.
6.  RNA Functions: mRNA carries genetic code from DNA to ribosomes; tRNA brings amino acids; rRNA forms the catalytic core of ribosomes; some RNAs act as enzymes (ribozymes) or regulatory molecules (siRNA, miRNA).
