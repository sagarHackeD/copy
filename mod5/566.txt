566. Uniqueness of identity and inverses

(i) Identity is unique:  
Suppose (e_1) and (e_2) are both identity elements in (G). Then
[e_1 = e_1 e_2 = e_2,]
so they are equal.

(ii) Inverse of each element is unique:  
Let (a Ɛ G), and suppose (b) and (c) are both inverses of (a). Then
[b = b e = b(ac) = (ba)c = e c = c.]
Hence the inverse is unique.
