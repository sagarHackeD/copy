54. Intersection and union of subgroups
Prove (H n K) is a subgroup:  
Let (H, K ≤ G).
- Identity: (e Ɛ H) and (e Ɛ K → e Ɛ H n K).
- Closure: If (x, y Ɛ H n K), then (x,y Ɛ H → xy Ɛ H), and (x,y Ɛ K → xy Ɛ K). Hence (xy Ɛ H n K).
- Inverses: If (x Ɛ H n K), then (x Ɛ H → x^{-1} Ɛ H), and (x Ɛ K → x^{-1} Ɛ K). Hence (x^{-1} Ɛ H n K).
Therefore (H n K ≤ G).
Is (H U K) a subgroup?  
No, in general.  
Counterexample: Let (G = S_3), (H = {e, (12)}), (K = {e, (13)}). Then
[H U K = {e, (12), (13)}.]
But ((12)(13) = (132)), which is not in (H U K). So (H U K) is not closed under the group operation, hence not a subgroup.
