54. Explain electronic nose in food science.
Definition:
An Electronic Nose (E-nose) is a device that mimics the mammalian olfactory system to detect, identify, and quantify volatile organic compounds (VOCs) in gaseous samples. It comprises a sensor array, a signal processing unit, and pattern recognition software.
Principle and Working:
The principle is analogous to the E-tongue but for gases. Volatile compounds interact with sensing materials (e.g., metal-oxide semiconductors, conducting polymers, quartz crystal microbalances), causing a change in electrical properties (resistance, mass, frequency). The unique "smellprint" of the gas mixture is then analyzed using multivariate statistics.

```

```
Applications in Food Science:
| Application | Detailed Description |
|
| Freshness & Spoilage Detection | Monitoring the release of biogenic amines, ammonia, and sulfides in meat, fish, and dairy products during storage to determine shelf life in real-time. |
| Fermentation Monitoring | Tracking the production of specific volatiles (ethanol, acetic acid, esters) during the brewing of beer, wine, and soy sauce to optimize the fermentation process. |
| Authenticity & Adulteration | Detecting the geographical origin of wines, olive oils, and coffee by identifying distinct volatile profiles. Detecting adulteration (e.g., cheaper oils added to olive oil). |
| Quality Grading | Objective grading of tea, coffee beans, and cocoa based on aroma intensity and complexity, reducing reliance on human tasters. |
| Microbial Contamination | Early detection of mold, yeast, or pathogenic bacteria (like *Salmonella* or *Listeria*) via their unique metabolic volatile signatures, before visual spoilage is apparent. |
