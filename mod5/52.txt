52. Define Klein 4‑group and verify (A = {1, 3, 5, 7})
Definition:  
A Klein 4‑group is a group of order 4 in which every element is its own inverse (i.e. (x^2 = e) for all (x)). It is abelian and isomorphic to ({Z}_2 X {Z}_2).
Verification for (A = {1,3,5,7}) under multiplication modulo 8:
Compute squares modulo 8:
[1^2 ≡ 1,    3^2 = 9 ≡ 1,    5^2 = 25 ≡ 1,    7^2 = 49 ≡ 1.]
Thus every non‑identity element has order 2. The set is closed under multiplication mod 8 (products of odd numbers are odd and not divisible by 8, so they remain in ({1,3,5,7})). Associativity and commutativity follow from multiplication of integers. The identity is (1).  
Hence ((A, ▪ \bmod 8)) is a Klein 4‑group.
